# DKIST_SolO_Fibril

![Fibrils and Their Hotter Counterparts](./M3_vbi_hri_fieldline_transition.gif)

This repository contains the code used to analyze and generate the figures and movies in the paper "Active Region fibrils and their hotter counterparts" by Zhu et al. (2026), submitted to A&A. The figures are presented in the order they appear in the paper, with each figure's corresponding Jupyter notebook provided for reproducibility.

[Figure 1](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/fov_summary.html) shows the fields of view of VBI, ViSP, and IRIS/SJI overplotted on context images or magnetograms from SDO/AIA, EUI/FSI, SDO/HMI, and PHI/HRT.

[Figure 2](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/all_channel_layout.html) presents the multi-wavelength observations of the chromospheric fibrils and their hotter counterparts, including DKIST/VBI, SolO/EUI HRIEUV, SDO/AIA, and IRIS/SJI. The animated version of this figure is generated in [Online Movie 1](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/all_channel_layout_movie.html).

[Figure 3](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/app_magnetograms.html) shows the photospheric magnetograms beneath the chromospheric fibrils, inverted from Hinode/SOT-SP data. 

[Figure 4](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/vbi_fibril_alignment.html) presents linear MHS extrapolation optimized to match the orientations of the chromospheric fibrils observed in DKIST/VBI Hβ images. 

[Figure 5](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/vbi_hri_fieldline.html) shows the extrapolated magnetic field lines overplotted on the chromospheric fibrils and their hotter counterparts observed by HRIEUV. The animated version of this figure is generated in [Online Movie 2](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/vbi_hri_fieldline_movie.html). [Online Movie 3](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/vbi_hri_field_transition_movie.html) illustrates the continuous change in the apparent orientation of the extrapolated field lines as the viewing perspective shifts from Earth to Solar Orbiter.

[Figures 6 and 7](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/spacetime_vbi_aia_iris.html) show the space-time diagrams of the chromospheric fibrils and their hotter counterparts observed in DKIST/VBI Hβ, HRIEUV, SDO/AIA 171 Å, and IRIS/SJI 1400 Å.

[Figure 8](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/vbi_chase_line_width.html) shows the consistency between the CHASE Hα chromospheric line width and the presence of dark fibrils in DKIST/VBI Hβ.

Figure 9 (a handdrawn cartoon) illustrates the two scenarios of the formation of the hotter counterparts of the elongated fibrils. 

[Figure A.1](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/vbi_fibril_alignment.html) shows the segmentation and skeletonization of the chromospheric fibrils observed in DKIST/VBI Hβ images.

[Figure B.1](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/app_magnetograms.html) compares the photospheric magnetograms measured by SDO/HMI, DKIST/ViSP, PHI/HRT, and Hinode/SOT-SP.

[Figures C.1 and C.2](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/app_extrapolation_compare.html) compare the low-lying magnetic field lines traced from different extrapolation models, including the nonlinear force-free field extrapolation (NLFFF), and the linear magnetohydrostatic field extrapolation (MHS). The comparison is made in the context of the chromospheric fibrils observed in DKIST/VBI Hβ images and the force-free parameter $\alpha$ at the bottom boundary. The 50th-percentile separation angle, $\theta_{50}$, between the extrapolated field at a height of $\sim 1$ Mm and the orientations of Hβ fibrils is given at the top left corner of each panel.

[Figure D.1](https://yjzhu-solar.github.io/DKIST_SolO_Fibril/ipynb_html/hrieuv_discussion.html) shows an additional HRIEUV observation of fine transition region and lower coronal structures in another near-limb active region, to further illustrate the presence of the hotter counterparts of the fibrils are ubiquitous in active regions.
